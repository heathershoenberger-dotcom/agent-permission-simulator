      Step 3: The surprise

      Your agent was instructed to plan a trip. But consider these additional possibilities:

      • Calendar: Access might expose medical appointments or private meetings unrelated to travel.
      • Email: Searching confirmations might reveal messages from family, colleagues, or financial services.
      • Location: A precise location history can reveal sensitive patterns; a destination city may be enough.
      • Purchase history: Past spending may be used to infer a budget or personal characteristics.
      • Memory: Preferences can improve future trips, but stored inferences may outlast the original task.
      • Payment: Preparing a booking is different from authorizing a charge.

      Key distinction: Data access, collection, storage, onward sharing, and permission to act are separate design choices.
